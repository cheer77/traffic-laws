# Traffic Rules Engine — bounded Phase 5 implementation

`@traffic-laws/country-rules/traffic-rules-engine` is a pure deterministic evaluator of explicitly authored UA intersection facts. It imports no renderer, scene editor or UI code. `TrafficSituation` v1 remains backward compatible: new optional facts describe regulator and road-user absence, road equivalence, lane restrictions, signal mode, vehicle type, swept path width and occupancy intervals. Missing facts stay missing. The engine never infers legal facts from a 3D object's position, colour or orientation.

## Decision contract

`analyzeTrafficSituation(input, rules)` validates both inputs and returns maneuver admission (`allowed`, `prohibited`, `unknown`), directed yield obligations, pairwise priority relations, path conflicts, concurrent-candidate assessments, an explanation, exact rule IDs and diagnostics. A green admission is separate from right of way; a yield edge is separate from physical path overlap. No total driving order is produced. `technicalCertainty` reports algorithmic completeness; `certainty` additionally gates on normative review. Bundled PDR records are **unverified**, so current real-data results are `unsupported` for publication even when `technicalCertainty` is `determinate`. Only synthetic test fixtures carry mock `verified` metadata; they are not legal approvals.

`@traffic-laws/scenarios/scenario-decision` composes this result with the validated situation, claim and scene binding into `SharedScenarioDecision`. It contains participant IDs, scene instance IDs, lane and maneuver, admission, the authored route, yield targets, spatial/temporal conflict partners, rule references and diagnostics through `analysis`, claim support and evidence-labelled scene findings. `validateScenario` checks its `claimSupported` and conflict findings. The development question UI and 3D overlay receive **the same evaluated object**; neither React nor Three.js recomputes priority. The overlay adapter only turns its route coordinates and IDs into static display geometry.

`determinate` requires explicit supported facts, path widths and times where pairs exist, complete applicable records from the pinned official edition, and verified source metadata. `ambiguous` indicates contradictory control/sign/path facts or opposite obligations. `unsupported` covers an unimplemented rule, exception or unverified legal basis. `insufficient_data` identifies absent facts. An empty signal list is not evidence that signals are off, and an active signal contradicting declared unregulated control prevents priority evaluation. A result may contain provisional edges while its overall certainty is not determinate; consumers must show the certainty and must not turn those edges into a published exam answer.

Contradictory main/secondary declarations on lanes of one approach are `ambiguous`. A STOP sign is `unsupported` for full intersection admission because mandatory stopping is not implemented, even though a secondary-to-main yield relation can still be reported provisionally. These diagnostics are not a STOP-rule implementation.

## Geometry and time

The geometry stage intersects polyline segments in x/z metres, including endpoint contact and collinear overlap. For disjoint centerlines it calculates the minimum segment distance and compares it with the **authored** sum of swept half-widths. A missing width produces `unknown`, not “safe”; no AABB shortcut is used. Width must include the actual maneuver envelope and clearance, which requires author review. The time stage compares explicitly authored occupancy intervals; overlap means **possible** shared-zone occupancy, not a collision prediction. No speed, acceleration or animation physics is inferred. Legal yield edges are computed separately and do not arise merely from a geometric crossing. A simultaneous `possible` result is emitted only when overall certainty is `determinate` and the bounded authored model found no conflicting envelope; with unverified rules or unresolved conditions it becomes `unknown`. A yield duty can coexist with concurrent non-interfering paths. It is not a real-world safety guarantee. Non-overlapping time intervals are not called simultaneous.

## Scenario and scene boundary

An optional `Scenario.trafficDecision` binds a validated situation to a structured positive claim (`yield` or `prohibited`). `validateScenario` checks the authored correct answer, vehicle IDs, a technically determinate result, all applied rule IDs, exact provision labels in the explanation, and required vehicle/signal/sign scene bindings. It compares signal indications and expected asset kinds with the Scene Definition snapshot. For the unrotated urban preset, it also checks bound vehicle position/heading and authored route start/exit, plus sign/signal approach and heading. Other layouts are explicitly unconfirmed. Text-only tickets still validate without an engine decision.

The additive optional `trafficDecision.sceneBinding` v1 is scenario-owned and leaves Scene Definition v1 unchanged. Its strict runtime validator requires the actual road instance, one road link per semantic lane, an explicit inactive classification for every visible vehicle/character not active in the situation, and an explicit out-of-scope classification for every visible sign/light not bound to the situation. It rejects duplicate, missing and overlapping assignments. Signal and sign IDs/applicability come from the situation and are checked against scene asset identity, indication, pose and lane approach; a main/yield sign also cannot contradict an explicitly authored road class. `assessSceneBinding` labels visible pose evidence **observed**, author declarations **authored**, and absent or unsupported facts **unresolved**. Scene snapshots and binding objects are reconstructed on validation; older scenario JSON without a binding remains valid but reports missing scene evidence. There is no silent migration.

The Web development gallery uses neutral rings, static planned-path strips and endpoints; selection and conflict colors do not encode legal permission. In the three drafts, an inactive vehicle or character is inactive **only by author declaration**, and unbound signs/lights are explicitly out of the modeled question. The view displays technical certainty, unverified normative status, diagnostics and authored assumptions. These checks cannot prove free-text legal correctness, lane classifications, path widths/times or invisible conditions. All three examples remain hypotheses for human review, not production tickets.

The overlay adds at most seven small meshes to the urban scene in these examples, without animation-frame updates. R3F owns and disposes their mounted geometries/materials. No asset geometry, material, camera, light, Scene Composer or shared renderer was changed. The historical Phase 4 271-mesh/57,930-triangle/241-draw-call measurement is not a new benchmark; a comparable GPU/frame-time measurement remains necessary before treating the gallery as a performance baseline.

Priority-dependent tickets cannot transition to `verified` or `published` through the local validator even if review metadata is typed in. `publishScenario` remains a local editorial helper for non-engine content, not authentication. Production Web has no content feed; the scenario gallery is development-only. A trusted reviewer workflow must independently authenticate the reviewer and bind approval to an immutable scenario/situation/ruleset revision before production publication. No editable JSON flag can supply that proof.
